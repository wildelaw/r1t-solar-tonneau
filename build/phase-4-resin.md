# Phase 4: The Resin Pour (Direct Bond Method)

> **Status: Complete.** Steps were revised as the work progressed. Leveling the mold was done before the flood coat — it should be confirmed *before* any resin is mixed, not after.

## 1. Scuff & Wipe the Skin

Lightly sand the cured, painted back skin with 120-grit sandpaper and wipe it clean with acetone. This ensures the flood coat chemically bonds to the back skin and gives the cells a clean surface to sit on.

## 2. Level the Mold

Before mixing any resin, level the mold in both directions with a bubble level. The flood coat will self-level, so any tilt in the mold shows up as an uneven resin line and an off-center expansion gap. Once the mold is level, do not move it until the panel is fully cured.

## 3. The Seal Coat

Mix ~16 oz of epoxy and pour a thin skim coat over the fronts (blue side) of the solar cells, making sure every cell is fully covered. Use a brush to dab resin under the edges of the cells to push trapped air out.

The cells are fragile once soldered into a string, so do not lift them to chase bubbles underneath — assume the seal coat has wet them out and that residual air will rise out during the flood coat.

{% include figure.html image="/Images/4.1-Seal-coat.jpeg" caption="Seal coat poured over the cells, fully covering each one." %}

## 4. The Flood Coat & Expansion Gap

Wait for the seal coat to reach a gel state (around 3 hours) before flooding. Mix the flood-coat resin **16 oz at a time** — do not mix a single large batch, or the bucket will overheat and kick off early. Pour each batch over the cells and let it flow out.

Pour until the resin is **almost up to the top of the frame**, but *not* overflowing. Leave a 1/16" expansion gap below the top lip of the steel frame — the silicone cap will sit in this gap in Phase 5.

{% include figure.html image="/Images/4.2-Flood-coat.jpeg" caption="Flood coat poured 16 oz at a time, resin brought up to just below the top of the frame." %}

## 5. Heat-Gun the Bubbles

Once the flood coat is poured, do a single pass over the whole surface with a heat gun to pop any remaining air bubbles. Keep the heat gun moving and hold it back from the surface — you want to break the surface tension, not cook the resin. (A plumber's torch works too, but the heat gun is gentler on the cells and bus wires.)

## 6. Cure

Leave the panel perfectly level and undisturbed for 48 hours. The clay/plumber's-putty mounds under the bus-bar holes will prevent any resin from leaking out the bottom. Do not move the mold during the cure.
